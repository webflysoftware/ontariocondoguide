Every Ontario condominium corporation must file **annual returns** with the **Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO)**. It is easy to treat this as back-office paperwork — until a late fee invoice arrives or a buyer's lawyer flags a compliance gap on a status certificate review.

In January 2026, CAO opened **returns season** for the 2026-27 cycle with a **modernized filing platform**. The deadline to file is **March 31, 2026**. This guide explains what boards and managers need to know. Confirm current requirements on the [CAO filing page](https://www.condoauthorityontario.ca/boards-and-governance/filing-requirements/file-a-return/) — this is educational information, not legal advice.

## What this means

An **annual return** is a statutory filing that updates key corporation information on CAO's **public Condo Registry**. It is separate from your AGM, audited financials, and budget — but boards who ignore it create compliance and reputational problems.

Returns confirm details such as:

- Corporation legal name and registration information
- Service address for notice
- Director and officer information
- Management provider details where applicable
- Other registry fields CAO requires for the filing period

Some information appears on the **public registry**, which owners, buyers, and lenders consult. Inaccurate or stale registry data causes friction during sales, refinances, and owner disputes.

## Key dates for 2026

| Milestone | Date |
| --- | --- |
| Filing window opens | January 5, 2026 |
| Annual return deadline | **March 31, 2026** |
| Late filing fee trigger | After March 31 (or 150 days post-registration for new corps) |

CAO encourages corporations to **start early** in 2026 because the filing platform changed. Do not wait until the last week of March if your management company handles hundreds of corporations.

## What's new on the CAO platform

CAO announced several upgrades for the 2026 returns season:

- **Modernized interface** with clearer layout
- **Step-by-step navigation** and progress tracking
- **Simplified instructions** throughout the flow
- **Auto-fill** from previous filings and registry data
- **Canada Post address lookup** integration
- **CMRAO registry integration** for management details
- **Final review page** before submission
- **Real-time error handling** with specific correction prompts

If your corporation filed in prior years, expect some fields to pre-populate — but **verify everything**. Director changes, management switches, and address updates are common sources of stale data.

## Who does the work

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Board** | Ensures filing happens; confirms information is accurate |
| **Property manager** | Often completes the online submission |
| **Treasurer / secretary** | Supplies director and officer details |
| **Legal counsel** | May assist if registry disputes or registration issues arise |

Director accountability does not disappear because management clicks submit. Include CAO return filing on your **Q1 board calendar** alongside AGM planning. See [director duties](/guides/ontario-condo-director-duties) for broader compliance expectations.

## Fees and penalties

CAO invoices corporations after a return is submitted. Payment methods include credit, debit, Google Pay, and direct deposit. Corporations must pay **outstanding prior-year balances** before filing the next annual return.

**Late filing fee:** **$200** per return filed after the deadline.

**Late payment fee:** If fees are not paid within **90 days** of invoice, an additional **$50** plus **5%** of the outstanding balance and **2% monthly interest** apply.

These amounts add up quickly for corporations that defer compliance year after year — and they are avoidable with a simple calendar reminder.

## Pre-filing checklist

Before logging into the CAO portal:

- [ ] Confirm current **director names and positions**
- [ ] Confirm **officer** appointments (president, secretary, treasurer if applicable)
- [ ] Verify **management company** details match CMRAO records
- [ ] Confirm **service address** for notice is current
- [ ] Resolve any **outstanding CAO invoices** from prior periods
- [ ] Identify who has **portal access** and login credentials
- [ ] Allow time to learn the **new interface** if filing early in January

After filing:

- [ ] Save confirmation of submission
- [ ] Pay invoice promptly
- [ ] File **notices of change** promptly when director or management details change mid-year

## How this connects to meetings and governance

CAO returns are not a substitute for proper **AGM** process, **meeting notice**, or **director elections** — but they sit in the same compliance ecosystem. Buyers and owners increasingly cross-check registry data against meeting minutes and status certificates.

A corporation that runs a valid AGM but neglects CAO filings still signals governance weakness. Conversely, filing returns on time does not fix invalid meetings or quorum failures.

If you are planning your AGM calendar for 2026, pair this filing with the [AGM timeline calculator](/tools/agm-timeline-calculator) and [AGM checklist](/templates/agm-checklist).

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming management filed** without board confirmation
- **Waiting until March 30** during a platform transition year
- **Stale director data** after elections or resignations
- **Unpaid prior invoices** blocking new submissions
- **Wrong corporation selected** in multi-property management portfolios
- **Ignoring notices of change** after mid-year board turnover

## Bottom line

CAO annual returns are a mandatory, recurring obligation with a **March 31, 2026** deadline. The 2026 platform refresh makes early filing wise. Boards should confirm accuracy, pay on time, and treat registry compliance as part of sound governance — not an optional administrative task.
