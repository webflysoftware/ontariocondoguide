Running a condominium meeting is part parliament, part community event. The **chair** sets the tone: whether the meeting stays orderly, whether owners feel heard, and whether votes survive later scrutiny. In Ontario, chairs operate under the **Condominium Act, 1998**, the corporation's **declaration and bylaws**, and whatever procedural rules the board has adopted.

This guide focuses on practical chairing for **board meetings** and **owners' meetings** (including AGMs). It is educational information, not legal advice for your corporation.

## What the chair does

The chair's core jobs are the same in most Ontario condos:

- **Open and close** the meeting on time
- **Confirm quorum** before substantive business
- **Follow the agenda** and keep discussion on topic
- **Recognize speakers** fairly and enforce reasonable debate rules
- **Put motions to a vote** and declare results clearly
- **Adjourn** when business is complete or when quorum fails
- **Support accurate minutes** by stating outcomes aloud

The chair is not a dictator. They enforce process so the corporation can make valid decisions — not to silence legitimate owner questions or rush contested votes.

For board agenda structure, see the [Ontario condo board meeting agenda guide](/guides/ontario-condo-board-meeting-agenda). For AGM context, see the [Ontario condo AGM guide](/guides/ontario-condo-agm-guide).

## Before the meeting

Strong chairing starts before anyone enters the room.

### Know the meeting type

| Meeting type | Who attends | Quorum basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Board meeting** | Directors (management often present) | Usually majority of filled director positions |
| **AGM / owners' meeting** | Owners, proxies, guests as permitted | Units represented per declaration/bylaws — often 25% |

Do not mix procedures. Owner debate rules and proxy registration do not belong in a closed board session unless your policy explicitly allows observer periods.

### Review the package

The chair should read the full agenda, supporting reports, and any owner motions or requisition items **before** the meeting. Identify:

- Items needing a recorded vote
- Conflicts of interest requiring disclosure
- Contested elections or bylaw votes
- Whether in-camera segments are planned

Use the [board meeting agenda template](/templates/board-meeting-agenda-template) or [owner meeting agenda template](/templates/owner-meeting-agenda-template) as a starting point.

### Brief your team

For owners' meetings, coordinate with:

- **Registration** — unit list, proxy validation, name badges
- **Scrutineer(s)** — ballot handling and challenged votes
- **Management** — AV, timing, documents
- **Secretary** — minutes and motion wording

Run through quorum expectations with the [quorum calculator](/tools/quorum-calculator) and confirm the [AGM timeline](/tools/agm-timeline-calculator) if you are chairing an annual meeting.

## Opening the meeting

### Board meetings

1. Call the meeting to order at the scheduled time.
2. Confirm **director quorum** — note absences and whether any director participates remotely if permitted.
3. Approve or amend the agenda by motion if needed.
4. Approve previous minutes or defer if corrections are required.
5. Note **conflicts of interest** before related items — conflicted directors should not vote on those items.

### Owners' meetings

1. Call the meeting to order and introduce yourself as chair.
2. Confirm **proof of notice** was delivered (management or secretary states this for the record).
3. Complete **registration** — count units in person, by valid proxy, and by advance or electronic vote if used.
4. **Declare quorum** only after a reliable count. State the number of units represented and the threshold required.
5. If quorum is not met, follow adjournment procedure — do not proceed with elections or binding votes. See [what happens if quorum is not met](/guides/what-happens-if-quorum-not-met).

State clearly when the meeting moves from procedural items to substantive business. Owners appreciate knowing when debate and voting will begin.

## Managing debate and owner questions

Owners' meetings can become heated. The chair's job is to keep order without suppressing legitimate participation.

**Practical rules that work:**

- Require speakers to **identify themselves and their unit** when addressing the meeting
- Use a **speakers' list** rather than open shouting
- Limit follow-up comments to one round on contentious items
- Separate **questions for information** from **motions requiring a vote**
- Pause the meeting if safety or harassment becomes an issue

At **board meetings**, individual owner disputes should not dominate unless the board has scheduled a specific owner correspondence item. Refer routine matters to management and place systemic issues on a future agenda.

For in-camera procedure at board meetings, see the [board meeting agenda guide](/guides/ontario-condo-board-meeting-agenda#in-camera-sessions).

## Running votes

Every binding decision should follow a clear sequence:

1. **State the motion** in plain language — read it twice if owners may be confused
2. **Invite debate** within reasonable time limits
3. **Call the question** when debate is exhausted or a motion to close debate passes
4. **Explain the voting method** — show of hands, ballot, proxy, or electronic
5. **Conduct the vote** with scrutineer support for contested items
6. **Declare the result** aloud — for/against/abstain counts where appropriate
7. **Record the outcome** for minutes and use the [voting results record](/templates/voting-results-record) for elections

### Director elections

Elections deserve extra care:

- Confirm the number of open positions and term lengths before voting opens
- Explain nomination rules and whether floor nominations are permitted
- Never combine election votes with unrelated motions on the same ballot without clear instructions
- Have scrutineers verify challenged ballots against published rules
- Declare results only after scrutineers confirm the tally

See [how condo board elections work](/guides/how-condo-board-elections-work) for the full election sequence.

### Proxies at owners' meetings

The chair does not casually reject valid proxies. Train registration staff on consistent rules and document reasons when a proxy is excluded. See the [CAO proxy form guide](/guides/ontario-condo-proxy-form) for validity requirements.

## Adjournment and reconvened meetings

Adjourn when:

- All agenda business is complete
- Quorum is lost mid-meeting and cannot be restored
- Quorum was never achieved at the opening
- A safety or technical failure makes continuing impractical

State the **reason for adjournment** and the **reconvened date and time** if one applies. If owners must receive new notice, say so clearly — do not imply a vote taken without quorum is valid.

## Minutes and follow-up

The chair helps the secretary produce usable minutes by:

- Repeating **motion wording and vote results** at the meeting
- Correcting misstatements before the meeting ends
- Confirming **in-camera** sessions are summarized appropriately

Minutes should record decisions, not arguments. For board meetings, capture attendance, quorum, resolutions, and action items. For owners' meetings, record elections, quorum counts, and owner votes on motions.

See the [minutes glossary entry](/glossary/minutes) for what belongs in the permanent record.

## Common chairing mistakes

- **Declaring quorum without a verified count** — especially when proxies arrive late
- **Rushing contested votes** before all ballots are distributed or collected
- **Allowing vague motions** — "the board should do better" is not a valid resolution
- **Mixing information sessions with binding votes** without clear transitions
- **Ignoring conflicts of interest** at board meetings
- **Failing to adjourn** when quorum is clearly impossible — wasting owner time and creating dispute risk

Many of these overlap with issues in [common condo AGM mistakes](/guides/common-condo-agm-mistakes).

## Chair checklist

Before you gavel in:

- [ ] Agenda finalized and distributed
- [ ] Quorum threshold confirmed for this meeting type
- [ ] Registration, scrutineer, and AV roles assigned
- [ ] Proxy validation rules understood by registration staff
- [ ] Motions and election procedures reviewed
- [ ] Minutes template and voting results form ready
- [ ] Adjournment and reconvened meeting rules confirmed
